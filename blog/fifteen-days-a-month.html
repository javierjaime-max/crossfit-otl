Fifteen Days
a Month

What consistency looks like at CrossFit OTL.

By Javier Jaime, Founder and CoachSeptember 3, 20265 min read

Every name at the top of the Board at the front of our gym got there the same way. Somebody decided to come in, then decided again the next day, and kept deciding for a long time. There's no other way up to the top of that Board.

People ask what the secret is. They might want it to be programming or a supplement or a workout they've never tried before. It's none of those, though. It is frequency. It is consistency. The person who shows up three or four days a week for a year will do better than the person who shows up six days a week for just a month and then never comes back. We've seen it happen time and time again for nine years, first in our garage and now on the floor at our gym.

The CountWhat one class buys you

Think about what happens in an hour here.

  • InstructedShown how the movement is supposed to look.
  • WatchedSomebody whose job it is to watch you is watching you.
  • CoachedCorrected, cued, and scaled to where you are today.
  • EncouragedTold you can do it when you were not sure that you could.
  • ChallengedAsked for more than you would have asked for yourself.
  • CountedNoticed by name by people who expected you to be there.

That is six things, and you will get all six of them every time you walk in the door. You get none of them at home, though. No app, no program, and no plan reaches through the door that you did not walk through.

We don't come to you.

That's the deal. It's also why it's effective. Your three hundred check-ins is three hundred times that you got all six of these things.

The FadeWhy results run away from people

Here's what we see. Somebody starts out strong. Ten classes the first month. Then life gets busy, and then it drops down to five. Then it drops to zero. And they never decided to quit. They just stopped coming.

Three months later, they come back and start over. Same soreness, same first eight weeks of learning the movements again. They weren't lazy. They were intermittent, and intermittent means paying twice for something that you already owned.

The body keeps track. It does not care how hard the last workout was. It cares whether you came back. Adaptation is your body answering this one question: is this going to keep happening? If the answer is yes, it builds. If the answer is maybe, it waits. Most people who feel stuck are not doing the wrong workouts. They're giving their body a maybe signal.

Ninety DaysThe three-month gate

We've watched enough people to know where they fall off. Some quit after a week because the soreness is just too much. Some quit after a month because their ego took a hit watching a grandmother move better than them. Some fade out after three months because the urgency that got them to walk through the door wore off, and nothing was there to replace it.

After three months, things change. Make it past 90 days and you are very likely to make it to six months. Make it to six months and you'll probably make it to a year. The first three months are critical, and walking through the door is what's going to get you through it. Perfect eating doesn't do it. Intensity alone doesn't do it. Attendance and just showing up does.

The StreakFifteen days a month

This is why the Committed Club exists. Fifteen classes in a month, and our app helps you keep track of it. You get that month's sticker. Twelve months, then eighteen months, then twenty-four.

But fifteen is not a random number. It's a little under four days a week, which is the frequency where the lifestyle stops being something that you do and starts being something you are. It's also the number where the two things we care about the most begin to show up together: people who are still here a year later, and people whose lives visibly change. The Committed Club is our best predictor of both. It is a predictor, not a guarantee, though. The people on the list are the ones learning, adapting, and hitting personal records. And they are the ones who are also not going anywhere.

Miss a month and your streak goes back to zero. It's not punishment. It's just what motivates you to keep walking through the door.

The LedgerWhat it costs and what it pays

What it costs you

  • An hour you could have spent anywhere else
  • A morning you did not want to get up
  • Being sore and coming back anyway
  • Showing up on the day you felt your worst

What it pays you

  • A body that still works
  • Years of health you were not otherwise going to get
  • A room full of people who know your name
  • The one thing you can point at and say, this is what I did about it

Before CrossFit, I got hurt quite frequently. When I started, I still got hurt. In my 50s, I rarely do, though. And the difference is not that I found an easier program. The difference is that I never stopped going, and my body eventually fixed what it kept getting asked to fix. Deanie, Isabel, Nicholas, my family, each have their own version of that same story.

Your MoveJust show up

If you're new, don't try to win the first month. Pick your days and show up. Three days a week, same days, same class if you can. Tell your coach that is the plan so somebody besides you knows it. Then hit fifteen once and find out what it feels like.

If you have been here for a while and drifted, you already know the number. Come back before the streak means that you have to start over.

Just Show Up.

What's On The Line? Everything.
