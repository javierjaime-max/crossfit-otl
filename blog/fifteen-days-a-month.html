Fifteen Days
a Month

What consistency looks like at CrossFit OTL.

By Javier Jaime, Founder and CoachSeptember 3, 20265 min read

Every name at the top of the Board at the front of our gym got there the same way. Somebody decided to come in, then decided again the next day, and kept deciding for a long time. There is no other route up that Board.

People ask what the secret is. They want it to be the programming, or a supplement, or some workout they have not tried yet. It is none of those. It is frequency. The person who shows up three or four days a week for a year will beat the person who shows up six days a week for a month and then disappears. We have watched it happen for nine years, first in the garage and now on the floor.

The CountWhat one class buys you

Think about what happens in an hour here.

  • InstructedShown how the movement is supposed to look.
  • WatchedSomebody whose job is to watch you, watching you.
  • CoachedCorrected, cued, and scaled to where you are today.
  • EncouragedTold you can, when you were not sure.
  • ChallengedAsked for more than you would have asked of yourself.
  • CountedNoticed, by name, by people who expected you to be there.

That is six things, and you get all six every time you walk in. You get none of them at home. No app, no program, and no plan reaches through a door you did not open.

We don't come to you.

That is the deal, and it is also why it works. Three hundred check-ins is three hundred times you got all six.

The FadeWhy results run away from people

Here is what we see. Somebody starts strong, ten classes the first month. Then life gets busy and it is five. Then it is zero, and they never decided to quit. They just stopped.

Three months later they come back and start over. Same soreness. Same first eight weeks of learning the movements again. They were not lazy. They were intermittent, and intermittent means paying tuition twice for ground you already owned.

The body keeps a ledger. It does not care how hard the last workout was. It cares whether you came back. Adaptation is your body answering one question: is this going to keep happening? If the answer is yes, it builds. If the answer is maybe, it waits. Most people who feel stuck are not doing the wrong workouts. They are giving their body a maybe.

Ninety DaysThe three-month gate

We have watched enough people to know where they fall off. Some quit after a week because the soreness surprised them. Some quit after a month because their ego took a hit watching a grandmother move better than they do. Some fade out around three months because the urgency that walked them in the door wore off and nothing replaced it.

After three months, the odds flip. Make it past ninety days and you are very likely to make six months. Make six and you will probably make a year. The first three months are the narrow part of the funnel, and being in the room is what gets you through it. Intensity does not do it. Perfect eating does not do it. Attendance does.

The StreakFifteen days a month

This is why the Committed Club exists. Fifteen classes in a month and the app puts you in. You get that month's sticker. Twelve months back to back and gear starts showing up. Twenty-four and there is more.

Fifteen is not a random number. It is a little under four days a week, which is the frequency where the lifestyle stops being something you do and starts being something you are. It is also the number where the two things we care about most begin to show up together: people who are still here a year later, and people whose lives visibly change. The Committed Club is our best predictor of both. It is a predictor, not a guarantee. The people on that list are the ones learning, adapting, and hitting personal records, and they are the ones who are not going anywhere.

Miss a month and the streak goes back to zero. That is not a punishment. That is what the word means.

The LedgerWhat it costs and what it pays

What it costs you

  • An hour you could have spent anywhere else
  • Mornings you did not want to get up
  • Being sore, and coming back anyway
  • Showing up on the day you felt your worst

What it pays you

  • A body that still works
  • Years of health you were not otherwise going to get
  • A room full of people who know your name
  • The one thing you can point at and say, that is what I did about it

Before CrossFit I got hurt all the time. When I started, I still got hurt. In my fifties I rarely do, and the difference is not that I found an easier program. The difference is that I never stopped going, and my body eventually fixed what it kept getting asked to fix. Deanie, Isabel, and Nicholas each have their own version of that story. Every one of them runs on the same fuel.

Your MoveJust show up

If you are new, do not try to win the first month. Pick your days and keep them. Three days a week, same days, same class time if you can. Tell your coach that is the plan so somebody besides you knows it. Then hit fifteen once and find out what it feels like to be counted.

If you have been here a while and drifted, you already know the number. Come back before the streak means starting over.

Just Show Up.

What's On The Line? Everything.
