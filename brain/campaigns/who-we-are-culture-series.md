# Who We Are — the CrossFit OTL culture series

Two audiences, one message. Coaches hear the *why* and the *how*. Members hear the *who we are*.

Everything below is from the two recordings unless marked otherwise. Nothing is invented.

---

## The problem being solved: drift

Drift is the slow erosion of the culture that brought people here. It is not one person being lazy. It is people following what they see. If someone with 500 check-ins on the Board hangs out in the back during the whiteboard, the new person thinks that is how it works here.

The GP's framing: "We've done such a great job of creating a culture of our coaches that we follow the class plan. What may start to happen is the little things that cause drift end up becoming contagious."

Deanie's framing: "The coach is responsible to control the floor for the entire sixty minutes. If the coach does not communicate effectively with their voice, the class will drift. That is where the breakdown is happening."

**What we sell:** coaching. Not facilitation of a workout, not access to a gym. In a coached environment people are watched, given chances to improve, encouraged, challenged, and their names are spoken.

---

## The guardrail (second recording, recorded on purpose after the first)

Everything in this series is delivered with this in front of it:

- We are not a legalistic or dogmatic group that punishes people or singles them out.
- If you can't be on time, still come. If you have to leave early, you're still welcome. Some people will be five minutes late every day for a season because there's no way to get across town. We'd rather keep them than have them feel unwelcome.
- Talk to your coach or the staff. We'll get you integrated into class if you're late, and we'll put your equipment away for you if you have to leave early.
- The 0-to-60-minute expectation "is not about control, but about impact. Giving them the best value for the investment they are making in our business and our community."

Deanie's version for coaches: kind, friendly, firm. "Not bitchy, no RBF." Like getting kindergartners to line up. "Raise your hand if you're a 5 PM-er." If you bark "Deanie, come to the front," the rebel personality either won't come back to your class or walks over begrudgingly.

**Existing brain rules this must respect** (from `../ops/member-experience.md` and `../coaching/class-management.md`):
- Starting and finishing on time is **not a rule** and is never written as one. It is common courtesy to the class coming in behind you.
- The closing block is **Cool-Down & Recovery**, never "circle time."
- The five parts of the hour: Briefing → General Warm-Up → Specific Warm-Up → The Workout → Cool-Down & Recovery.
- Never bare "OTL" in member copy. Never route members to a person by name ("ask a coach").
- No guilt framing.

## The six chunks

Each chunk becomes one internal piece and one external piece. Same substance, different door.

### 1. For-time workouts: nobody leaves the floor

**What happens:** some athletes scale so far under the stimulus they finish minutes early, then drift to phones, the benches under the rig, or the front table. Three or four leave together and the floor splits in two.

**Internal (coaches):** this sits right next to putting weights away early as a gym no-no. Be diligent about saying *why* at the start of every for-time piece. The intended stimulus and the release are called out before 3-2-1.

**External (members):** *We are the people who wait on the floor until everybody's done.* Stay present, without standing over anybody. Some people don't want a crowd in their ear. Nobody finishes to an empty room. An early finish is also a signal to the athlete: there was a little more in you today. (GP, 2026-09-05: the message is presence, not a cheering section.)

### 2. The Briefing: the hour starts here

**What happens:** the coach starts the whiteboard and a couple of people walk up while the rest are in the back putting on shoes and talking. Long-time members feel like they've heard it before.

**Internal (coaches):**
- The Briefing is the start of your leadership for the hour. The most compliance you will ever get is in the first few seconds. Lose it there and it goes downhill for sixty minutes.
- Prep starts a few minutes before the top of the hour. "Class starts in a few minutes. Raise your hand if you're 5 PM. One minute, meet me at the whiteboard. Grab a jump rope on the way." You are already in control when the clock hits.
- Do not start until you have eyes and ears. Call people forward. Waiting twenty seconds is fine. Being intermittent is not, because the drift is contagious.
- What you deliver has to be worth walking up for: stimulus, scaling, safety, logistics, the things in the coach's notes the member can't see from reading the workout.
- Every coach does this the same way. We cannot have "this coach cares, that one doesn't, so I'll go to the one who doesn't."

**External (members):** *We are the people who show up for the whiteboard.* Being on time is how you respect the other athletes. The Briefing is where the coach tells you what the workout is supposed to feel like and how to scale it. That's the part you can't get from reading it on your phone.

### 3. Coach presence: sixty minutes, on the floor

**Internal (coaches):**
- The table up front exists for the equipment we use to start and end class. It is not a place to stand during the workout. Deanie: "a crutch."
- Managing by walking around. You don't have to say anything. Walk, put eyes on individual athletes, look for one thing to compliment or correct. If you start a clock and check out until it ends, the room is handed to the civilians.
- Deanie: "If you're behind the coach's table or on your phone during the WOD, you cannot expect to have command of the room when the workout is finished. If people are putting equipment away while you're coaching, that's on you. That's a coaching issue, not an athlete issue."
- Deanie: "If you are not exhausted after coaching, you didn't do it right."

**External (members):** this one is mostly shown, not said. If it gets a line: *You're paying for a coach, and the coach is going to be right there.*

### 4. FORD: know your people

**Internal (coaches):** Family, Occupation, Recreation, Dreams. If you know that about your people, they feel seen and they come back to places where people know their name. FORD is also how you tell the difference between a rough day and drift. Someone who's had a combative teenager the night before, a divorce, a spouse traveling all week, four straight days and sore: they show up and move their body. That's fine and it is not commented on. "I know where you are. I see you. I'm glad you're here." Somebody who stays at the 12-inch box every single class when they can do 20 is a different conversation.

**External (members):** probably not a standalone piece. It shows up as the warmth in every other piece.

### 5. Everybody helps everybody: shared work

**What happens:** the coach releases the weights, and the person with two change plates is done in ten seconds. The person who went RX has six heavy plates, a bar at the far end of the room, and is still on the floor from being at the edge of the stimulus. The Cool-Down starts before they're done, so people join the coach and leave them to it.

**Internal (coaches):**
- Say it out loud. We've had this in our heads as family and never said it. "Everybody help everybody put away their weights. Cool-down starts after all the equipment is away."
- We don't move to the next part until everyone is there. Same as the warm-ups.
- Pick a different person each class to help you put equipment away, and go do it yourself.

**External (members):** *We are the people who help everybody put away their weights.* The order matters (GP, 2026-09-05): the coach says it's time, you put your weights away, then you look for ways to help clear the floor. Deanie's phrasing (2026-09-05): "Don't put your weights away until the coach says it's time." Somebody's plates, the clips, the chalk. Keep working until everybody's done, then the cool-down starts.

### 6. Stimulus: a little more than last time

**Internal (coaches):**
- Chronic under-scaling is drift too. If someone finishes far under the stimulus every time, encourage them to move between tracks. Not RX. Two and a half pound plates. Five pounds. An extra round.
- If lots of people are finishing well under, we're not doing our job in the Specific Warm-Up on setting the level of work.
- Occasional under-stimulus is fine. Repeated, every class, is the conversation.
- The GP's why: "Our best referrals are people whose lives change. Change is adaptation. Adaptation happens from challenge. Results take progressive loading."

**External (members):** *We are the people who give a little more than last time.* Not "add the two-and-a-half" on anything printed; members don't know what that means (GP, 2026-09-05). The plate example lives only inside body copy where there's room to explain it.

## The identity statement (the GP's words, close to verbatim)

> We are the people who wait on the floor until everybody's done.
> We are the people who help everybody put away their weights.
> We are the people who show up on time for the whiteboard and respect the coaches.
> We are the people who give a little more than last time. *(fourth line added in drafting, 2026-09-05; GP has not struck it)*

"If we are that, and there's somebody who doesn't want to be that, likely those people weren't going to stay long anyway."

This is the spine of the printed piece and the anchor post. The chunks above hang off it.

## Sequence

1. **Coaches first.** Nothing goes to members until every coach has heard the six and the guardrail. The GP's consistency point depends on it: members must not meet the standard from one coach and not another.
2. **The identity statement**, printed and posted in the app, the week after the coach clips go out.
3. **One member post per week** in the app, in the chunk order above. Chunk 3 (coach presence) and chunk 4 (FORD) may stay internal.
4. **The email** goes out with the identity statement, once, with the guardrail paragraph in it.

## Open questions from the second recording (the Pocket summary raised these; the GP has not ruled)

- **Fairness:** how do coaches hold the standard for the room while making room for the person who is five minutes late every day, without the room reading it as special treatment? Likely answer from the recording itself: the exception is arranged with the coach ahead of time, so it isn't a surprise to anyone.
- **Safety threshold:** when is a late arrival too late to join the workout as programmed? Missing the Specific Warm-Up is the obvious line. Needs a GP ruling before it goes in a coach piece.
- **Coach capacity:** who puts equipment away for the early-leaver in an 18-person class? Chunk 5 answers it if the room does it.

## Voice rules for drafting (see memory: `feedback_javier_voice_pass_consistency_post`, `feedback_ai_cadence`)

Contractions. Plain statements instead of punchlines. His images, not mine. No em-dash appositives. Would he say the line out loud at the whiteboard? If it sounds like a quote, cut it. Expect his read-aloud pass on every member piece.
