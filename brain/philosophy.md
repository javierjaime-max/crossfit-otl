# CrossFit OTL — Philosophy & Voice

## The Core Belief

CrossFit OTL was never built around competitive fitness. It was built around **life change**.

> "My story in CrossFit has never been about competitive CrossFit. I'm competitive with myself. The things that brought us to where we are have been about lifestyle change, life change, health restoration — and those things happened not only with me but with my wife, my daughter, my son, my mom, my sister-in-law. All people whose lives were affected because I found CrossFit 10–12 years ago."

The ripple started with Javier. It spread to Deanie. Then to Isabel and Nicholas. Then to the garage group. Then to the affiliate. That's the model — and it's the story OTL tells.

---

## What OTL Values (Unwritten, But Real)

- **Know your members.** Every coach has a photo on file. When someone walks in, call them by name — intentionally, every time.
- **Care about the why.** When someone comes in, understand why they're there. Don't customize the programming, but customize the message — what will actually benefit this person.
- **The lifestyle, not just the workout.** CrossFit is not a place you work out. It's a way of being. The daily feedback from your body about what you ate and how you slept — that feedback loop is the lifestyle.
- **Family is the core audience.** Not competitive athletes. Families. Adults with teens. Kids. Homeschool communities. The gym was literally built for a family to run and for families to attend.
- **Professionalism over friendship.** A slight separation between coaches and members is intentional. Alcohol blurs lines. Party culture creates instability. OTL stays out of that. Care for everyone — but stay on the outside of friend groups cycling in and out.

## On Coaching

> "When you make it so simple for kids, then when you teach an adult — the more complicated you make it, the harder it is. Teaching kids is actually better training for coaching adults."

- Isabel coaching kids at 14 made her a better coach for adults.
- The L2 changed how Javier thinks about class structure — specifically the idea that a strength workout *can be* the whole hour. CAP programming builds this in.
- Class management: the rotation model (one person lifts at a time, coach sees every rep) vs. the split-rig model (half the class goes, then the other half) — used depending on the movement and the group's comfort level.
- The 5am, 6am, and 6pm class crowds are the Mid-Cities working family. They show up. They do the work.

## On Nutrition

The whole family has been through: Zone → Paleo → Intermittent Fasting → Keto → Carnivore.

Key moments:
- Deanie's GERD, skin issues, headaches all cleared within days of going Paleo. Surgery cancelled.
- This is what committed the family — not competition outcomes, but measurable health results.
- Isabel's senior thesis on Ancel Keys and the sugar industry — the entire food pyramid was built on corrupted science, political capture, and corporate greed.

OTL's nutrition philosophy: teach the why. Don't just tell people what to eat — explain the mechanism. Isabel and Nicholas grew up reading the same books, articles, and CrossFit Journal pieces Javier was reading. They know it because they lived it.

## On Business

- **Two-Brain framework.** Business is not intuition — it's systems. Two-Brain gave OTL the playbook, pricing, sales process, and mentoring it needed to go from garage to affiliate without amateur mistakes.
- **CAP programming.** Not writing your own programming doesn't make you a lesser affiliate. CAP has clear RX/Intermediate/Beginner tracks built in. New coaches learn from every class. Members from other gyms consistently comment on the class *flow*.
- **The no-sweat intro.** Every new member comes in for a conversation before they join. Not a trial class. A conversation. See if it's a fit — for them and for OTL.
- **Pricing is not the conversation.** If someone's comparing OTL to Planet Fitness, OTL can't help them. That's okay.

## OTL's Voice (How to Sound Like the Gym)

- Direct. Not preachy.
- Personal. Stories over statistics — then statistics to back the story.
- Family-first. Not "performance." Not "athletes." People.
- Honest about the hard part. CrossFit is not easy. That's the point.
- Never motivational-poster soft. "You've got this" is not an OTL phrase.
- Credentialed. Every coaching cue has a reason. The L3/CCFT path is the proof.

**Things OTL doesn't do:**
- Compete with Planet Fitness on price
- Promise quick results
- Party with members
- Blast homeschool identity as a marketing angle
- Pretend CrossFit is for everyone (it's for anyone willing — not everyone)
