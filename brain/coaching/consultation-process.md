# CrossFit OTL — The Consultation Process

This is the full consultation framework as Javier describes it from inside the room. It covers why people come in, how to read them, how to walk through the conversation, and how to present the program.

---

## The Starting Point: Courage It Takes to Walk In

When people new to CrossFit drum up the courage to come in, they have gone through quite a bit of deliberation before making the appointment. That context shapes everything about how the consultation opens.

If someone is an existing CrossFit athlete looking for a new gym, the conversation is simpler — they've already bought in. Most of the time they just want to know pricing.

But if someone hasn't worked out in a while, or is switching from a different workout style, or came on a friend's recommendation — by the time they get into the office they have had to work hard to get there. Treat that accordingly.

---

## Opening Question: "What Prompted You to Make the Appointment Today?"

This is the first question. It often reveals a specific trigger event in their life.

Common answers:
- "I've just been thinking about it for a while." → Follow up: "How long have you been thinking about this?"
- They finally decided to do something
- They finally have enough time or enough money
- A medical condition
- A family member who got sick — they realize they need to change course

The first couple of answers reveal whether their reason has urgency behind it. When it does — when something has actually happened — their need to do something drastic is real, not casual.

---

## Goals: Expect Vague Answers First

Ask what they hope to accomplish in the next 12 months if they were committed to a program.

Most give vague answers: "I just want to be healthier." "I want to be more fit." "Do a couple more things."

Some will eventually disclose they want to lose weight — and some are ashamed to say it even though it's something they need. If someone hasn't given a clear answer, prompt them: "Do you have any specific body composition goals? A specific weight, body fat percentage, running time, amount to lift?"

Usually people haven't identified those specifics yet. Do not press them to answer if they don't have them. If they don't have specific goals yet, there's more pain to surface in the consultation by continuing.

---

## Nutrition: Where the Real Picture Comes Out

Move toward what they do on a daily basis for nutrition. Walk me through it. Specifically: what did you have for breakfast, lunch, dinner?

People will tell you the best version of what they think they should say — "I focus on protein, I eat at home." Then they'll disclose: "But I'm busy, I don't plan, I end up eating fast food" or "I don't have enough time and then I don't eat at all."

This is where you find out whether they're actually making good choices consistently.

The key insight: it's not the few good choices that are going to move the needle for them. It's the fact that on a regular basis they're full of habits that are leading them away from their goal. They ultimately don't reach their goal because they have not switched their lifestyle. They have some general knowledge about some things to do but they don't implement them.

Ask them to give themselves a score on their nutrition. Most people who walk through the door give themselves a C or below — they are in need of help. Occasionally someone gives themselves an A or B — those people are just looking for a place to work out, not a nutrition and accountability program.

By the time someone sits down across from you, you can usually see whether they are in significant need of help. The question is whether they know it, and whether they know what to do, and whether they know what it's going to cost.

---

## The Attendance Hook: The Sticker

If someone shows up 15 or more times in a month, they receive a sticker. Everybody loves a sticker. But the most important thing about the sticker is what it represents: if they're committed and they show up, they will get results.

---

## Presenting the Programs

### On-Ramp (New to CrossFit)

Tell them directly: "If you're new, I don't want you jumping into the group fitness class. We need you to come participate in our on-ramp program."

The on-ramp is one-on-one coaching. It covers:
- Getting acquainted with what we do in CrossFit
- Evaluating their movement patterns
- Teaching core fundamental movements
- Walking through what a class looks like
- Vocabulary and methodology

The reason for one-on-one rather than throwing them into a class: they may have a negative experience if they don't understand the lingo, or haven't acclimated to the intensity levels they'll encounter in class.

### Nutrition Coaching and Accountability App

After on-ramp, they need their nutrition dialed in. OTL has an app for tracking nutrition habits and fitness habits, with daily or weekly dialogue with a nutrition coach. The coach keeps them on track on establishing habits, then executing on those habits weekly and monthly.

### The Lifestyle Reset (Primary Recommendation)

This is the 12-week program. It includes:
- 3 on-ramp sessions (done in week one)
- 9 additional one-on-one private training sessions distributed throughout the 12 weeks
- Total: 12 personal training sessions — averaging one per week
- Unlimited group classes
- 4 InBody scans (every 4 weeks)
- Nutrition coaching and accountability app
- Nutrition consultation with the nutrition coach

The total retail value is over $2,500. For people who commit to the full program and pay upfront: **$1,420**.

If they can't pay upfront, offer **three monthly installments**. Be clear: these are not monthly dues with the ability to cancel. This is a commitment to the full $1,500, paid in three installments of $500 at the beginning of each four-week period.

### Entry-Level Path (Budget-Constrained)

If they can't do the Lifestyle Reset, and they're new to CrossFit:
- 3-day on-ramp: $270
- Unlimited monthly classes: ~~$170~~ → **$180 per 4 weeks** (corrected by GP 2026-08-04)
- Total first month: ~$440

Tell them: if they paid that for two more months at $500 each, they'd get the equivalent of the full package. It's a pathway in.

## What the Consultation Is Actually Doing

The goal is to understand what's holding them back from accomplishing their health goals. Usually it's:
- They don't know what to do
- They don't have a place to do it
- They need coaching
- They need accountability
- They need motivation

The combination that solves this: group fitness (community, coaching, motivation, encouragement to work hard) plus one-on-one coaching (individual connection, refining specific challenges) plus the nutrition accountability program (produces the visible results — the physical changes that sustain motivation).

Without the nutrition component, someone can gain strength and metabolic conditioning from 90 days of CrossFit and not see anything physically change on their body. That's demoralizing. When they also lose 10, 15, or 20 pounds, they look different, feel different, and perform different. The nutrition piece makes the other results visible.

We have testimonies of people whose lives changed — 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 pounds lost. The job of the consultation is to get them close enough to hear it and commit to the program that actually delivers it.

---

## Why "Reset" Not "Transform"

The program is called a Lifestyle Reset, not a total transformation. The distinction matters and is worth explaining:

For somebody to transform totally, it's not instantaneous. It's a stair-step effect. They do something focused for maybe 90 days — the 12-week reset. When they're done, they may relax. Some habits will stick, some won't. Their new normal at 24 or 36 weeks down the road is a better version of themselves, but not the final version.

Over time, there may be another point where they need to reset again. In that next reset, they establish another higher-level habit. When they come off of it, some stick, some relax. The accumulation of this over years results in a genuinely different person in body — not in 12 weeks, but in 12 years — if they accumulate lifestyle resets on a regular basis.

The point is: instead of selling instantaneous results, OTL is doubling down on the idea that CrossFit is the cure. There is no quick fix. Show up for the next 10 or 12 years and you will be fitter, stronger, healthier. The Lifestyle Reset is the program for people who are struggling with focus, accountability, motivation, knowing what to do — or fighting the mental demons that keep them from making the commitment.
