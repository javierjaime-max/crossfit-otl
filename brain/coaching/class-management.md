# CrossFit OTL — Class Management and Weekly Workout Planning

## Daily Class Schedule

Monday–Thursday and Saturday–Sunday:
5AM · 6AM · 7AM · 8:30–9:30AM · Noon · 4PM · 5PM · 6–7PM

**Friday exception:** 4–5PM only. No 5PM or 6PM class on Fridays.

---

This is Javier's thinking process when reviewing the weekly programming before it runs. It covers space allocation between adults and teens, stimulus management, equipment constraints, and the coach's role in preparation.

---

## The Primary Variable: Space Allocation Between Adults and Teens

OTL runs adult and teen classes simultaneously in the same facility. The first planning decision each day is: who works out where?

**General rule:** move the class with the smaller footprint or no outdoor-running requirement to the back. If adults need outdoor runs, they need the back door — and if the movement after the run is small (dumbbells, no rig), the adults can work fully from the back while teens use the front.

**What drives the decision:**
- Does the workout require the rig? (Keeps the class in the front.)
- Does the workout require outdoor runs? (Need the back door — consider moving that class to the back.)
- How many barbells does each class need? (Barbell sharing between classes is a real constraint.)
- How many people in each class? (High-volume adult classes can consume most barbells — affects teens if they're also lifting.)

**Example logic (from a real week):**

Monday adults had 400m runs, dumbbell bench press, ring pushups, hand release pushups. No barbells, no rig, small footprint. Eight dumbbell benches fit in the back. → Adults move to the back.

Monday teens had deadlifts + toes to bar + back squats. Needed barbells and the rig. → Teens stay in the front.

**The complement check:** once you've decided who goes where, confirm that the two classes don't create conflicts on equipment or traffic (e.g., adults walking through teens' working area for outdoor runs).

## Four-Time Workouts: Stimulus Management

For time workouts require the coach to manage finish times carefully. Key concerns:

1. **Slow people fall outside the stimulus on repeating efforts.** If someone runs a 400m at a pace that takes 3 minutes on the first round, they'll be slower on rounds 2–4 and far outside the intended stimulus.

2. **The intermediate version often finishes faster than the RX version.** Intermediate scaling typically drops reps or weight, not rounds. This means intermediate athletes finish quicker — if you have borderline-RX athletes, they may need to be capped or guided to scale.

3. **Class flow between back-to-back classes.** If the 5am class runs long, the 6am class can't start on time. This is not just class management — it's the rhythm of the whole morning. The 5am must finish on time, every time.

The coaches' notes in the app provide the intended stimulus (target time, expected rounds, scaling guidance). The pre-class coach job is to know this and communicate it at the start, not to wing it.

## Equipment Constraints by Movement Type

**Barbells are the binding constraint.** A large adult class (15–18 people) doing clean and jerks can consume most barbells. If teens are programming back squats the same session, there's a direct conflict. Plan for it explicitly.

**Echo bikes and rowers:** Limited bikes — won't cover the class if it's large. Rowers typically go along the east wall. When calories are "any machine," both are valid but rowers require setup time and spacing.

**Sandbags:** OTL currently only has 100-pound sandbags. If a workout calls for sandbag carries, most women can't do the prescribed weight. Without lighter sandbags on hand, the movement needs a substitute. Flag this ahead of time — don't discover it at 5am.

**Boxes:** Can be dual-purpose. If the workout has single-leg squats (scaling to a box) and step-ups, the same box serves both movements — coordinate the station setup accordingly.

## Preparing Coaching Notes Before Class

Best practice: sit down and do all three weeks of posted workouts in one hour, rather than the night before each class. One focused hour produces three weeks of notes. Way more efficient. Whoever is responsible for coaching notes (e.g., Clay) should own this in blocks, not individually.

What coaching notes must include:
- Full gym setup — where the whiteboard goes, where the coach's table sits
- Safety reminders for the setup — cones if people are on the rig with barbells nearby, any safety separations needed
- Score-logging instructions — coach must know exactly what to tell members at circle time about how to log their score
- Reminder for time cap / equipment-away protocol — for time workouts: nobody puts equipment away until everyone is done; for AMRAPs, this usually isn't an issue

**The 5am coach should know the full setup, the intended stimulus, and the score-logging protocol before the first member walks in.** Not during warm-up.

## Barbell Sharing for EMOM / Every-4-Minute Workouts

When a workout has heavy barbells and multiple people per barbell, the timing cascade matters.

Example: every four minutes × four sets, deadlifts + farmer carry. If you're sharing:
- Partner 1 goes at the top of the minute
- Partner 2 goes at the two-minute mark
- Partner 3 goes at the three-minute mark

This only works if each person's working window is short enough to not overlap. For the example above (8 deadlifts + 200-foot farmer carry), each person's work takes roughly one to two minutes — sharing among two is feasible, among three requires tight sequencing.

**200 feet indoors is not realistic.** For farmer carries, mark outside. Set heavy dumbbells or kettlebells just outside the back door — come inside to do the deadlifts, carry outside.

## The Scheduling Rationale: Teens and Adults Together

Adults and teens work out simultaneously, on opposite sides of the gym. For teens who can't drive yet, the 5pm slot runs at the same time as the adult 5pm class. Parents can see their kids working out across the room. Kids can see parents.

This is intentional and not logistical — see `philosophy.md` for the design reasoning.

The class management challenge is the direct operational result of this design: every day, two different programs for two different populations need to coexist in the same space without equipment conflicts or traffic conflicts.

## Class structure and closing block (GP correction, 2026-08-04)

The five parts of the hour: **Briefing → Warm-Up → Skill or Strength → The Workout (WOD) → Cool-Down & Recovery.**

⚠️ **We do NOT call the closing block "circle time."** It is an **intentional time at the end of every workout for cool-down stretching and recovery**. During it the coach also gives **announcements** and a **preview of tomorrow's workout**.

**"For time" workouts — the release rule:** when a workout is scored "for time," people finish at different times. **Nobody puts equipment away until everyone has finished AND the coach tells the class** they may put it away.

**Starting/finishing on time is NOT a "rule."** The GP explicitly rejected calling it "the 5AM rule." Classes start and finish on time because the next class is waiting — frame it as **common courtesy**, never as an enforced policy.
