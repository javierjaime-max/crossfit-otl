# Nick — Member Profile

> Note: This is not Nicholas Jaime (Javier's son). This is a separate member named Nick.

---

## Basics

- Mid-30s
- Architect by career
- Single, never been married
- Has a son, Maddox, approximately 11 years old — from a relationship in his 20s
- Lives near the gym (OTL is convenient to his home)

---

## Family Background and Motivation

Nick's parents divorced when he was young. His father had a history of infidelity. Nick still has an active, engaged relationship with his father but also carries the awareness that his father did not set a good example as a husband or father.

Nick's clear motivation: he wants to be a better example for his son than his father was for him.

This is complicated by the fact that Nick has only partial custody. Maddox's primary custody is with his mother, who lives 45 minutes to an hour away. This creates a constant tension — Nick loves OTL's community deeply, but his love for Maddox regularly pulls him toward that other city to be actively present for his son.

Maddox is highly athletic — involved in football, baseball, wrestling, and attends OTL CrossFit kids classes. Nick takes significant pride in his son's athletic development and posts regularly about their time together.

---

## Why He's at OTL Specifically

Nick sees Javier's relationship with his own son Nicholas, sees the fitness level and the values Javier models as a father, and recognizes in that the kind of father-figure example he wishes his own father had provided. He wants to give Maddox the legacy his own father didn't give him — and he uses Javier's family as a reference point for what that looks like.

His phrase for it: he wants to "establish the legacy with Maddox and increase the OS baseline." He can see that baseline established in other places (notably the Jaime family) and wants to raise it for his own family.

What he loves about OTL: it is a family gym. He knows that Javier and his wife raised their children with values aligned to what he wants for Maddox.

---

## Membership and Performance

- Has been a member for approximately three years
- Committed Club member, has reached one-year status
- Started with the on-ramp program. Coaches who assessed him saw he moved very well from the start — given high scores of capability.
- Took to the programming completely. Among the top performers on the male leaderboard on a daily basis.
- Has never questioned payments — though he's asked for help a couple of times on his son's program fees, which suggests his finances, despite a professional career, are likely tight from joint custody costs.
- Not arrogant. Described as genuinely humble despite his performance level.

---

## Vulnerability and Character

During his time at OTL, Nick has entered and left a relationship in which he displayed some of the same attributes he judges in his own father. He has had to reckon with that. This is noted not as gossip but as context for who he is — a person actively working to be different from a pattern he inherited, who doesn't always succeed, and who is aware of the gap.

---

## Retention Risk

Nick's primary retention risk is not commitment — it is life circumstances. His love for Maddox and the demands of non-primary custody could require him to relocate to be closer to his son's school and activities. If Maddox's mother moves, or if proximity becomes a requirement, Nick may have to leave.

This is the pattern with die-hard members who eventually leave OTL: they don't leave because they've stopped caring or because the gym failed them. They leave because their life circumstances relocate them. Nick fits that profile.

---

## Archetype Value

Nick is a useful archetype for the consultation and assessment process because he represents the high-commitment, community-embedded male member in his 30s for whom the gym is both a fitness practice and a fatherhood statement. His retention driver is not fitness goals — it's identity and legacy. That's a different type of member than someone chasing weight loss, and it has implications for how OTL communicates its value to similar people.
