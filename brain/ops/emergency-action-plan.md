## Section 3 — Medical Emergency Protocols

### 3.1 — Cardiac Arrest (HIGHEST PRIORITY)

Cardiac arrest is the scenario that kills fastest and where every second of delay matters. This protocol was triggered by the Bob Douglas event and is the most important procedure in this document.

**Signs:** Member is unresponsive. Not breathing normally (gasping or no breath). Collapsed and cannot be roused.

**Execute in this order — no pauses:**

1. **Call out loudly.** "Someone call 911 NOW. Get the AED NOW." Point at specific people — do not say "someone" generically. "You in the red shirt — call 911 and stay on the line. You — get the AED from [location]."
2. **Start CPR immediately.** Place heel of hand on center of chest. Press down 2 inches. 30 compressions at a rate of 100–120 per minute (beat of "Stayin' Alive"). Minimize interruptions.
3. **If trained in rescue breathing:** 30 compressions, 2 breaths (1 second each, visible chest rise). If not trained or unsure: compression-only CPR is effective — keep going.
4. **When AED arrives:** Turn it on and follow voice prompts. Do not stop CPR until pads are on and AED says "analyzing." Resume immediately after shock is delivered.
5. **Keep going.** Do not stop CPR unless: EMS takes over, the person shows clear signs of life (breathing normally, movement, responsiveness), or you are physically unable to continue (rotate with another person every 2 minutes if possible).
6. **Send someone to the front door** to flag down EMS and lead them directly to the victim. Tell them the entry route.
7. **Clear the floor.** Move other members to the far wall or outside. Calmly — do not create panic. "Everyone please move to the back — give us room."
8. **Do not move the victim** unless they are in immediate physical danger from their location.

**After EMS arrives:** Step back, answer their questions, and follow their direction. Provide the victim's name and any known medical history if available.

### 3.2 — Serious Injury (Musculoskeletal, Laceration, Head)

**Signs:** Member is in significant pain, unable to bear weight, visible deformity, deep laceration with bleeding, or loss of consciousness after impact.

**Steps:**

1. Stop the class. Do not tell members to keep working while you assess — the workout is over.
2. Get down to the member's level. Do not move them if there is any possibility of spine involvement (fall from height, impact to head/neck).
3. Call 911 if: the member is unconscious, there is major bleeding that won't stop with direct pressure, you suspect broken bone (especially spine, femur, pelvis), or the member asks you to.
4. For lacerations: gloves first (first aid kit location — **NEEDS_JAVIER**). Direct pressure with clean cloth or gauze. Do not remove pressure.
5. For suspected fractures: immobilize, do not attempt to straighten. Ice if available. Call 911 or have a responsible adult drive to ER if the member is stable and insists on self-transport.
6. Complete the Incident Report Form within 24 hours (Section 6).

### 3.3 — Diabetic Emergency (Low Blood Sugar / Hypoglycemia)

**Signs:** Member suddenly confused, shaky, sweating, pale, weak, or acting drunk without cause. May happen mid-WOD.

**Steps:**

1. Stop member from continuing the workout.
2. If conscious and able to swallow: give 15g of fast-acting sugar immediately. Options: 4oz orange juice, regular soda (not diet), glucose gel (if available in first aid kit), or several pieces of candy.
3. Have member sit or lie down. Stay with them.
4. Wait 15 minutes. If no improvement, give another 15g of sugar.
5. If member becomes unconscious or cannot swallow: do NOT give food or liquid. Call 911.
6. Ask the member if they have a glucagon kit — if yes, ask them to direct you or locate a family member who knows how to use it.

---

### 3.4 — Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis

**Signs:** Hives, swelling of face/lips/tongue/throat, difficulty breathing, rapid pulse, dizziness after eating, drinking, or insect sting.

**Steps:**

1. Ask immediately: "Do you have an EpiPen?" If yes, help them get to it or administer it per their instruction.
2. Call 911 for any reaction with breathing difficulty or throat swelling — this is anaphylaxis and it can kill within minutes.
3. Have member lie down with legs elevated unless breathing is easier sitting up.
4. If they have an EpiPen and you administer it: note the time. Call 911. A second reaction can occur 8–12 hours later — they need hospital monitoring.

---

### 3.5 — Seizure

**Signs:** Sudden loss of consciousness, rhythmic muscle jerking, stiffening, confusion, staring.

**Steps:**

1. Clear the area around the member — remove barbells, boxes, any hard objects within reach.
2. Do not hold them down. Do not put anything in their mouth.
3. Cushion their head with something soft.
4. Time the seizure.
5. Call 911 if: seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, another seizure follows, the member does not regain consciousness, the member is injured, the member is pregnant, or this is their first known seizure.
6. After the seizure ends: roll them gently onto their side (recovery position). Stay with them — confusion and disorientation after a seizure is normal and can last 10–20 minutes.

---

### 3.6 — Heat Emergency

**Signs (Heat Exhaustion):** Heavy sweating, cool pale skin, fast/weak pulse, nausea, muscle cramps, fatigue, dizziness.

**Signs (Heat Stroke — Emergency):** High body temperature (103°F+), hot/red/dry skin, rapid/strong pulse, confusion, unconsciousness.

**Steps for Heat Exhaustion:**

1. Move member to a cool area immediately — outside if shade + breeze, or near fan/AC.
2. Have them lie down, legs elevated.
3. Apply cool wet cloths to skin. Have them drink cool water if conscious.
4. If no improvement in 15 minutes, call 911.

**Steps for Heat Stroke:**

1. Call 911 immediately — this is life-threatening.
2. Cool them as fast as possible: cold water immersion if available, ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, fan with cool water misting.
3. Do not give fluids if unconscious.

## Section 4 — Non-Medical Emergency Protocols

### 4.1 — Fire

**Evacuation Route:** **NEEDS_JAVIER — mark primary and secondary exits on floor map**

1. Pull the nearest fire alarm if present (and if the fire is real — do not activate for smoke from burning food, etc.).
2. Call out clearly: "Everyone out NOW — [primary exit] / [secondary exit if primary is blocked]."
3. Coaches sweep the facility: locker room, bathrooms, back area. Last person out confirms the space is clear.
4. Assembly point: **NEEDS_JAVIER — designate a specific outdoor meeting location**
5. Call 911 from outside the building.
6. Do not re-enter for any reason until fire department clears the building.
7. Account for all members and coaches at the assembly point.

---

### 4.2 — Severe Weather / Tornado

DFW is in Tornado Alley. Take this seriously.

**Monitor conditions:** The lead coach on any morning or evening shift should check weather before class if there are storm systems in the area. If a Tornado Watch is in effect, brief members at class start.

**Tornado Warning (confirmed tornado):**

1. Move everyone immediately to the most interior room or area of the building — away from windows and exterior walls.
2. If the building has no interior room, move everyone to the lowest point against an interior wall, cover heads.
3. Do not go to vehicles. Do not try to outrun a tornado.
4. Interior shelter location: **NEEDS_JAVIER — designate and mark**
5. Remain sheltered until the warning expires or you receive an all-clear.

---

### 4.3 — Active Threat

**If a threat enters the facility:**

**Run:** If there is a safe escape route, get out. Leave belongings. Help members who can run. Go — don't wait.

**Hide:** If you cannot run, get out of the threat's line of sight. Lock or barricade doors if possible. Silence phones. Stay quiet. Do not open the door for anyone claiming to be a rescuer until law enforcement confirms it is safe.

**Fight:** If you have no choice and your life is at immediate risk, fight with everything. Use barbells, dumbbells, kettlebells. Throw things. Make noise. Multiple people acting together can overwhelm a single threat.

**After you are safe:** Call 911. Give location, number of threats, description, last known location, and weapons seen.

## Section 5 — After Any Emergency: Post-Incident Protocol

**Within the hour:**

1. Ensure all injured parties have received or are receiving care.
2. Notify Javier and Deanie if they were not on site. Do it immediately.
3. Secure the scene — do not disturb evidence if law enforcement may be involved.
4. Collect names and contact information of any witnesses.

**Within 24 hours:**

1. Complete the Incident Report Form (template below). Be factual — time, what happened, what you observed, what actions were taken. No speculation.

## Section 7 — Required Coach Certifications

Every coach scheduled to lead a class must hold a current certification in:

- CPR/AED (American Red Cross, American Heart Association, or equivalent)
- CrossFit Level 1 (minimum)

Certifications expire. It is each coach's responsibility to renew before expiration. Javier and Deanie will audit certifications at the start of each year.

A coach whose CPR/AED certification has lapsed may not lead class alone until it is renewed.
