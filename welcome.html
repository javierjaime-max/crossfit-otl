Why We Exist

This was built around
life change.

That's what CrossFit did for the Jaime family, and then for everyone who walked in after them.

Javier found CrossFit in 2012 and it changed him. Then Deanie. Then Isabel and Nicholas, then the neighbors training in their garage. This gym exists because that kept going, and you're the next person in it.

What did the changing is ordinary and repeatable: movements you already use. Squatting, picking things up, pressing overhead, carrying, running. Done well, done at an intensity that's right for you, and repeated until your body adapts.

Every workout we program scales. The 62-year-old grandmother and the former college athlete do the same workout on the same day, in the same hour, with different loads, different reps, and different movements.

What you can expect from us

  • A coach on the floor, every class. Someone credentialed watches your reps and corrects them. All eleven of our coaches hold a CrossFit Level 1 or higher, seven hold a Level 2 or above, and one is a Certified CrossFit Trainer (CF-L3).
  • Programming with a reason behind it. We run CrossFit Affiliate Programming, with Beginner, Intermediate, and RX tracks built into every session. You'll always know which one is yours.
  • The whole picture. Training is one input. Nutrition, sleep, and consistency are the others, and we coach all of them.
  • Progress you can check. InBody scans, benchmark workouts, and lifting numbers you can pull up from six months ago.
  • A room where your family belongs. Kids and teens train while adults train, same building, same hour, year-round.

What we won't do

We won't promise you quick results. We won't compete with a big-box gym on price, because it isn't the same product. And we won't tell you this is easy, because it isn't.

Why We Exist03
The Methodology

So what is CrossFit?

CrossFit's own definition is one line: constantly varied functional movements performed at high intensity. Here's what each part of it means.

Constantly varied

The workout changes every day on purpose. Your body adapts to what it can predict, so we don't let it predict. We're training broad readiness rather than one narrow specialty.

Functional movements

Movements that move large loads long distances, quickly, and that mirror what life asks of you: stand up, pick it up, put it overhead, carry it somewhere.

High intensity

Intensity is relative to you, and it's where results come from. Your intensity on day one and on day four hundred are different numbers doing the same job.

The goal is work capacity across broad time and modal domains: being good across short and long efforts, across lifting and gymnastics and cardio. Life doesn't tell you in advance what it's going to ask for.

Go learn it yourself — don't just ask us

We'll answer any question you have, any time. But the members who understand why we program the way we do train better for it. All of this is public and most of it is free.

The CrossFit Level 1 Training Guide

The actual textbook, free to download at crossfit.com. Mechanics, the nine foundational movements, nutrition, programming, and scaling. Almost no members read it, and the ones who do train differently afterward.

The CrossFit Journal

The archive of foundational articles, including "What Is Fitness?" (2002), which lays out the ten general physical skills, the three metabolic pathways, and the sickness-to-fitness continuum this gym is built on.

crossfit.com and the CrossFit YouTube channel

Daily programming, movement standards, and demo videos for every lift and gymnastics skill we run. Good place to review a movement the night before.

Take the Level 1 course yourself

You don't have to want to coach. Some of our strongest members took the L1 just to understand the why. Talk to Javier or any of the staff. Every one of us has been through it and will tell you honestly whether it's worth your weekend.

Your Coaches05
The Hour

How a class runs.

Sixty minutes, five parts, every time. Arrive ten minutes early for your first few.

  1. Briefing

    Your coach walks the class through the day: the workout, what it's supposed to feel like, the target, and how each track scales. If you don't know which track is yours, ask here.

  2. General Warm-Up

    Coach-led, not specific to the day yet. Heart rate up, temperature up, joints moving through full range.

  3. Specific Warm-Up

    Now we rehearse the day's exact movements at light load. This is where your coach sets your weight and your scaling, and where technique gets corrected before it counts.

  4. The Workout of the Day

    Some days that's conditioning, some days it's a Heavy Day. Your coach is moving the whole time, correcting positions, adjusting loads, holding people to the intended pace.

  5. Cool-Down & Recovery

    Cool-down stretching and recovery, programmed as part of the session. Your coach also uses this time for announcements and a preview of tomorrow's workout.

Heavy Days

Some days the strength work is the workout, with no conditioning piece after it. That's deliberate. Cramming a lift in before a metcon every day means you never really train strength, you train a rushed version of it with one eye on the clock. Heavy Days give you room to build strength and power.

Three things new members always ask

"What if I'm last?"

Someone is. The class stays with you, and nothing gets put away until everyone finishes and the coach says so.

"What if I can't do the movement?"

Then you do the version you can do. That's what scaling is. Your coach will hand it to you before you have to ask.

"Do I need to be fit first?"

No. That's like showering before the shower. Come as you are and we'll start there.

How Class Works06
What’s Coming

Your first ninety days.

People quit CrossFit at predictable moments, for predictable reasons. None of it means the training isn't working. We're telling you now so you recognize it when it shows up.

WK 1Soreness

You will be sore in places you didn't know existed.

In a coached class you work harder than you would alone, using muscles you haven't used in years. This is the most common week to quit. It passes, and coming back sore helps more than resting.

WK 4Ego

Someone you didn't expect will out-lift you.

A grandmother, a stay-at-home mom, someone half your size. It stings. They started before you did, which is the whole difference.

WK 4Impatience

Thirty days won't show you what you came for.

This is a long-adaptation program, and the scale is a slow and unreliable way to see it. Track your lifts, your reps, and your InBody instead. Those move first.

MO 3The Fade

The urgency that got you here wears off.

Ten classes the first month, five the second, none the third, and you never decided to quit. This is how it ends for most people. The fix is a standing appointment: pick your days now, put them on the calendar, and treat them like work.

Make it past three months and the odds turn in your favor: six months becomes likely, a year becomes probable. Everything we do in your first ninety days is aimed at getting you through that gate.

The Committed Club

Attend fifteen or more classes in a month and the app puts you in that month's Committed Club, and you get that month's sticker. Keep stacking them and gear comes at the milestones: twelve months, twenty-four months, and beyond. That's fifteen classes a month, every month, for a year. Then two.

We track it because it's the best predictor we have of two things: who is rooted in this community, and who is going to set personal records and measurably improve their fitness and health. It's not a guarantee, but the people in the club month after month are the ones whose lives change.

The other thing that happens: if you're open to it, you'll make real friends here. Isabel and Clay met on this floor. Other members just ended up standing in each other's weddings.

Programs

What's available to you.

Group class is the backbone. Everything else exists to get you into it well, or to get you further once you're in.

On-Ramp

If you're new to CrossFit, you start here, one-on-one rather than in a group class. Three sessions covering the fundamental movements, how we scale, the vocabulary, and what intensity should feel like.

Group Class

Coach-led, every session scaled three ways. This is where the work happens and where the community lives. Two membership options: Unlimited, or a 12-session plan if you train a set number of days a week. Ask a coach which one fits your week.

The Lifestyle Reset — 12 weeks

What we recommend to most people who walk in wanting a change rather than a gym membership. Twelve weeks, built around one-on-one attention:

  • 3 on-ramp sessions in week one, then 9 more private sessions across the remaining weeks — 12 total, roughly one a week
  • Unlimited group classes throughout
  • 4 InBody scans, one every four weeks, so progress is measured
  • Nutrition consultation plus ongoing coaching and accountability through our tracking app

Development Time

Included with Unlimited. Extra floor time for your own strength and skills, worked around whatever class is in session. Not open gym.

Personal Training

One-on-one sessions for a specific goal, a specific lift, or a return from injury. On its own or alongside group class.

Nutrition Coaching

Habit tracking with regular dialogue with a coach. Training is one hour a day; nutrition covers the other twenty-three.

InBody Scan

Your first one is free, and a coach walks you through the results: your water, lean mass and body fat, and why lean mass drives your metabolism. After that it's $35, and it's open to anyone.

Programs09
Kids & Teens

Your kid trains
while you train.

Most gyms run kids classes after school as a separate program. We don't, and it's the most deliberate design decision in this building.

Kids and Teens classes run at the same hours as adult classes. When you're training on one side of the floor, your kid is training on the other. You arrive together, you work at the same time, you leave together.

And we run them year-round, on the same calendar as the adults rather than a sports season.

Why it's built this way

Kids install their defaults from their families, not from their coaches. A kid whose fitness is tied to football season stops training when the season ends, because the training belonged to the team, the coach, and a schedule someone else set. Most of those kids never come back to it as adults. They peaked at seventeen.

A kid who trains while mom and dad train associates fitness with the family instead of the season. When school sports end, nothing changes here.

Let athletics shape ability. Let the lifestyle shape the body and the long-term default. This gym is built for the second job.

The kids who stay through their teens, and who come back as adults, are almost always the kids whose parents were in the room.

The Floor

House rules.

Short list. All of it is about safety, respect for the space, and not making the next class worse.

Class has priority — 100%

A class in session gets first claim on all floor space, all equipment, and the coach's attention. Development Time works around the class, never the other way around.

Wait for the coach's call

On "for time" workouts people finish at different times. Nobody breaks down equipment until everyone is done and the coach tells the class to put it away. Then bars stripped, plates racked by size, everything else home.

Clean up blood immediately

Blood on a bar, a box, or the floor gets cleaned before you move on. Sweat and chalk, wipe down as a courtesy to whoever is on it next.

Chalk stays in the bucket

Use it over the bucket, not over the floor. It makes a real difference on a rubber floor.

What you may never drop

Never drop an empty barbell, kettlebell, or dumbbell, or a bar loaded only with 10-pound plates. Change plates, the small rubber-coated metal ones, are never dropped, and belong on a bar only alongside a larger-diameter rubber plate. If a weight is too heavy to set down under control, it's too heavy today.

Scale it and log it

Every score gets logged, RX or intermediate or beginner. Untracked training is a guess.

Kids stay on the brown floor

Children stay on the designated brown wooden floors, never on the black rubber. The only exception is a family-friendly session. We are not childcare — parents are responsible for their kids at all times.

Tell your coach about injuries

Before class, not after the workout. Old surgeries, bad shoulders, pregnancy, a rough night of sleep: all of it changes what we program for you that day.

Stay for the cool-down

Recovery is programmed, and it's when you'll hear announcements and tomorrow's preview. Leaving early means missing both.

Cheer for the last person

Non-negotiable. That person is working harder than anyone still standing.

Know This Before You Need It

In an emergency.

We've had a member go down on this floor with a heart attack. The people around him did the right things, but nobody could tell the 911 dispatcher where they were. Read this page once, today.

1

Our address — say it to the dispatcher

9155 Blvd 26, Suite 270
North Richland Hills, TX 76180

It's posted inside the front door and at eye level on the back door. The back one matters on running days and any time class is working outside. Look at both this week so you know where they are. A dispatcher can't send anyone until they have an address.

2

The AED is by the water fountains

Mounted on the wall, right by the fountains. Walk over and look at it today so you're not hunting for it under pressure. Once it's open it talks you through every step out loud. You don't need training to use it, and using it early is what saves people.

For everything smaller, a torn hand, a scrape, a bad shin, ask your coach for the first aid kit. Don't dig for it and don't tough it out.

3

If someone goes down

Don't freeze and don't crowd. Three jobs, assigned out loud:

  • Someone calls 911 and gives the address above.
  • Someone gets the AED from the water fountains and brings it over.
  • Someone goes outside to flag the ambulance and walk them in.

Your coach runs the scene. If you're not doing one of those three jobs, clear the space and keep the floor open.

Point out the AED and the door address to the next new person who walks in. That's how it gets known, member to member.
